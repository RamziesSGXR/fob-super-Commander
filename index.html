Field manual

HOW TO PLAY

The job. You command a loyal faction of Super Earth: a training camp that turns recruits into troopers and ships them to the front. You land with a command post, a flag, open ground, and a loan from the Bureau that paid for all of it. Grow the base, pay the Bureau back, and outrank three rival factions. Nothing ends the game: a base you leave alone stops growing but keeps running, and a bad day can always be climbed out of.

Landing. Pick a spot on the planet map. Water and cliffs can't be built on, but they shut the base in like a wall. Seeps of E-710 and ore fields are marked on the map; in the game, the RESOURCES view by the minimap shows them. You start with an outpost charter (the dashed square); bigger charters come later. Then choose where the HQ stands: it moves free until your first road.

The screen. Across the top: supplies, credits and what you owe, each with its change per watch (a day is six four-hour watches). On the left, the task board: your next orders and everything waiting on your desk, with the buttons to deal with it there. Bottom right, the minimap (click to go there) and the info views: resources, supply routes, roles, money, power, supply, condition and crew. Along the bottom, the build toolbar and the status bar, with the windows: ECONOMY, DESK, DEFENCE, EXPANSION, TRUTH, SCIENCE and STANDINGS.

Building. Draw roads first (ROADS, drag; G changes snapping). Buildings snap to plots along roads, door to the street. The toolbar is sorted by what things are for, and every card says what the building does and costs a watch. Lay the foundation first (landing pad, supply depot, barracks, mess hall, firing range): these bring the money in, and the rest of the base opens once they are placed. Draw a wall round it all (DEFENCE, Wall); a road through a wall is a gate. Large buildings have three designs and add-on parts, and can be refitted later.

Zones. Everyday buildings grow by themselves: paint zones along your roads from the ZONES bar (drag). Life Support grows bunks, mess halls and latrines; Logistics grows depots and fuel depots; Civic and Commerce grows vending machines, the Workers' Club and the franchise you pick. Each grows one building at a time when the base needs it (the DEMAND bars), at the standard picked on the bar, and never spends you below 500 supplies. Bunks follow jobs: crew posts, training places, gate guards and patrols, and the Ministry's growth target. Training, Operations and Industry zones grow nothing, but buildings you place in their own zone work 5% better. Buffer is kept clear. Keep bunks away from noise (ranges, motor pools, generators) and fuel, which cost morale; storage near a landing pad holds 15% more. The ZONING view shows it all.

Utilities. Power, water, sewage and fuel each cover a circle round the building that provides them (the UTILITIES bar; the UTILITIES view shows every circle). A building that draws power outside every power circle goes dark: generators, solar arrays, the HQ and Substations carry the grid. Bunks, messes, wards and the hydro dome need water and sewage: the HQ covers the first tents, then Water Wells, a Water Purification Unit by a river or the sea, and Water Towers to carry it further, with a Sewage Lagoon (smelly, and it contaminates wells within six tiles) or, at Fort, a Waste Treatment Plant. Bunks without them lose morale and risk an outbreak: troopers spend a watch or two with the medics, and a staffed Medical Bay halves the risk. A Fuel Farm pipes fuel to every generator, motor pool and vehicle bay in reach.

Engineers and standards. Nothing goes up by itself: engineer crews of three do every building job, one job per crew. Two crews land with you; an Engineer Yard takes three recruits and trains another in a watch. A new base starts with a security sweep of the landing zone, and building waits until it is declared secure. Beyond the landing zone the ground is covered in scrub and debris; crews clear it before they build on it, and CLEAR SCRUB (Roads bar) sends a crew to clear a patch for the salvage. Buildings come in three standards, picked at the top of the build bar: tents (cheap, fast, smaller, wear out and nobody is proud of them), prefabs, and permanent concrete (dear and slow, the most room, morale and propaganda; needs a Fort charter). Select a building to UPGRADE it a standard in place; it runs at half pace while the crew is in.

Money. Supplies build, pave and pay daily upkeep and the morning deliveries. Credits pay citizen wages, research and the market. You land owing the Bureau for the Founding Charter; it takes a little back every day. ECONOMY has the budget (what comes in and goes out, by source), your loans, and what each kind of building earns or costs. Credits may run into overdraft for a fee; past the limit the staff go unpaid.

The market. Every base on the planet trades supplies, rations, fuel and ammo for credits (ECONOMY, MARKET). Prices move every day, and your own trades move them against you. Rival bases post offers on the task board: they pay over the market for what they are short of and sell cheap what they have too much of.

The routine runs itself. Recruits arrive with the 07:00 pelican and train on the range, the course and the parade ground. Trained troopers ship out at 16:00 to fill the Ministry of Defence's orders. Trucks and forklifts carry rations, fuel and ammo along your roads (the SUPPLY ROUTES view shows where). The day follows the Army: First Call 06:00, Reveille 06:30, Retreat 17:00, Taps 21:00.

Standing. Three Ministries rank you each day, and the better your rank, the bigger the allocation:

  • DEFENCE: ship troopers, run patrols and special operations, keep the wall watched and the gates manned.
  • EXPANSION: build, fill the base, keep supplies flowing, repay the Bureau.
  • TRUTH: morale, propaganda and buildings in good repair, judged at the daily inspection.

Commitments. You can also buy standing: ECONOMY, COMMITMENTS sets what the base gives each Ministry every day, from NONE to ALL OUT. Defence takes ammo and rations, Expansion supplies and fuel, Truth credits and supplies. Each Ministry has a planet-wide index, like a share price (it shows under the standings): when every base is chasing a Ministry its favour costs more. No base can afford ALL OUT on everything, so pick what to chase and trade for what you lack.

The desk. Between deliveries there is always paperwork. Citizens and officials file requests. The Ministries call for goods by a deadline: set them aside or pay for ignoring them. Trooper files need grading. All of it shows on the task board and in DESK. None of it is required, but it all pays.

Land. The base sits on a 13.8 km section of the planet, cut into 600 m parcels. You land, raise the flag, set down the Command Post (the HQ and its own landing pad) and start with the 3 × 3 parcels round the flag. ROADS → LAND buys the parcels next to yours: each card shows the buildable ground, water, rock, E-710 and ore, and the price. Build anywhere on your land, inside the wall or out; the wall is for defence.

Landscape. The ground has height: hills, shelving shores and cliffs round the high rock. Buildings level their own footprint as they go down (not on ground that rises too much across it), and roads need a gentle grade. LANDSCAPE has Shift (raise with the left button, lower with the right), Level (to a height you type or Shift-click to sample), Soften and Slope (click two points), plus surface paints: sand, dry ground, lush grass and dirt. Earthworks cost supplies; dig below the waterline and it floods, and ground worked too steep is bare rock.

Time. At 1× a game hour takes three real minutes (2× and 4× with keys 2 and 3). A day is six four-hour watches: upkeep, food, fuel and pay are settled every watch, while the allocation, the standings, the inspection and the Bureau run once a day. Troopers keep the Army's day, with a night watch on duty so the base never quite sleeps.

Growing. A Comms Tower lets you run more landing pads, and a Science Lab researches upgrades. Four charters (EXPANSION): an Outpost (12 parcels), a Forward Operating Base (20 parcels: 30 votes, Expansion 50, 600 credits and 600 supplies), a Fort (36 parcels: 60 votes, Expansion 60, 1,200 credits and 1,500 supplies; permanent buildings and the larger ones) and a Major Base (every parcel: 110 votes, Expansion 70, 2,000 credits and 3,000 supplies). Each raises the land you may hold and the daily allocation.

Controls. Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom, Q E or right-drag to rotate. R turns a building, Shift-click places several. Space pauses, [ ] or 1 2 3 set the speed. Tab steps through the build toolbar, I through the info views. B opens ECONOMY, L the DESK, M the last window, C the comms log. P is photo mode, H hides the interface. Sound and volume are in the MENU.
