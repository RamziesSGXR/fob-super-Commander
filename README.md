# FOB Super Commander

*Build your base where managed democracy needs to be managed.*

Build and run your faction's base for Super Earth. A browser game for the **Super Earth Special Forces** community, in the same look as [Orbital Fury](https://orbitalfury.com/) and [Super Orbital Simulator](https://ramziessgxr.github.io/super-orbital-simulator/).

> **Prototype 0.11** (the version shows on the title screen and in the menu). Pick a landing site on a real stretch of planet, land in debt with a Founding Charter, draw the roads and the wall around the water and rock, build along them, train troopers, and grow from an Outpost to a Fort to a Major Base.

You command a loyal sub-faction of Super Earth: your name, your crest, your colours, your base. The base turns recruits into troopers for the front. Every week the Federation hands out supplies, and the better your standing with its Ministries, the bigger your share. Three other loyal factions on the planet want the same supplies.

## ▶ Play

The game is at `https://ramziessgxr.github.io/fob-super-Commander/`, served from the `main` branch. Work in progress lives on `dev` until it is pushed live. It runs in a browser on a computer, or on a phone or tablet held sideways. There is nothing to install.

## How to play

Like a city builder, the base runs itself: you lay it out and it gets on with it. Nothing needs a click to keep moving.

- **Found your faction.** Name it, name the base, pick a motto. The crest maker has 11 shapes, 17 symbols (including the Helldiver fist), 9 patterns, up to four letters and four colours, or upload your own logo (up to 2 MB, shrunk to 128 px, kept on your device). Pick your troopers' uniform, helmet and armband, and whether they wear the crest on the shoulder. Change any of it later from **MENU**.
- **Recruits in, troopers out.** Every morning recruits arrive with the supply pelican and fill your empty bunks: more housing, more recruits. From 08:00 to 20:00 they train on the Firing Range (fastest, needs an instructor and ammo), the Obstacle Course, the Parade Ground or in the Citizenship Hall. At 100 points they're troopers. At 16:00 a transport takes trained troopers from the landing pad to meet the Ministry of Defence's deployment order, paying 40 supplies each.
- **Supply lines.** Rations, E-710 fuel and ammo arrive on the supply pelican at 07:00, enough to fill your storage, billed automatically. Forklifts (one at the HQ and one per Supply Depot) and trucks (two per Motor Pool) carry them from the pad to storage, and from storage to the mess halls, generators, firing ranges and patrol offices that use them. Every vote eats a ration a day, at a mess (24 seats), a franchise (16) or the HQ (6). Run short and votes go hungry, generators stop and the range falls silent.
- **Difficulty.** Chosen in the faction editor. Recruit: a smaller, cheaper Charter, slower rivals, softer penalties. Veteran: the standard posting. Helldiver: a bigger Charter at a steep rate, hungry rivals and a Bureau that remembers everything.
- **Choosing a landing site.** After founding, pick where to land on the planet's region map. Each biome (desert, ice, jungle, moon, swamp, temperate) has its own ground. Water and solid rock can't be built on, but they close the base like a wall; roads cross water as bridges (three times the price). Seeps of E-710 and ore deposits are marked: an E-710 Extractor or an Ore Mine has to sit on one. The survey under the map scores each spot, and **SUGGEST A SITE** cycles the best six.
- **Placing the HQ.** After landing you choose where inside the charter the HQ stands (the flag goes beside it). You can move it free until you draw your first road; or just start building where it landed.
- **Starting out.** You land with just the Command HQ and the Super Earth flag, 3,600 supplies, 600 credits, and a Founding Charter of 3,000 credits owed to the Bureau. Draw a road, put a Landing Pad on it (supplies and recruits arrive at 07:00), then a depot, a mess and a barracks. Until a wall closes you can build anywhere; the Ministry gives you two weeks before it starts marking the missing wall.
- **The landing sweep.** A new base opens with a pelican dropping your engineers and a SEAF security detachment on the landing zone. The detachment sweeps a ring round the HQ until early afternoon and files an after-action report (with some salvage). Building waits until the zone is declared secure.
- **Engineers.** Nothing builds itself: engineer crews of three (hard hats, hi-vis) do every building job and every upgrade, one job per crew, oldest first. Two crews land with you. An **Engineer Yard** (Logistics) takes three of your recruits and trains them as another crew in a day; if the yard comes down, its crew is posted elsewhere. Idle troopers lend a hand and speed the work a little. The build bar shows how many crews are at work and how many jobs are open; a building's card says which crew is on it or how many jobs are ahead of it.
- **Scrub and debris.** Beyond the landing zone the ground is covered in scrub, rubble, ice or slag, depending on the biome. Anything built on it is cleared first (half an hour of crew time a tile, 5 supplies of salvage each). **CLEAR SCRUB** on the Roads bar sends the next free crew to clear a patch of up to 24 tiles for the salvage; click an ordered patch to cancel. Roads and walls bulldoze their own way through.
- **Construction standards.** Buildings with living or working space come in three standards, after US base camp doctrine: **Tent** (initial: 40% of the price, a third of the build time, 75% capacity, wears out unless maintained, half the morale and less propaganda), **Prefab** (temporary: plywood huts and containers, 70% of the price, 90% capacity) and **Permanent** (semi-permanent: the full building, the most room, morale and propaganda; needs a Fort charter). Pick one at the top of the build bar. Select a building and press **UPGRADE** to raise it one standard in place: you pay the difference plus a 15% fee, and it runs at half pace while the crew is in. Your HQ starts as tents. Bases from before 0.13 are all permanent.
- **Outpost, Fort, Major Base.** The Bureau's charter decides how much ground you may use, drawn as a dashed line. An Outpost gets 36 tiles across. With 40 troopers trained, Expansion standing of 55 and 1,500 credits you can be promoted to a Fort (56 tiles, a bigger allocation); with 110 troopers, 70 and 4,000 credits, a Major Base (the whole 80-tile map). Promote from the Expansion tab. Some large buildings need a Fort or better.
- **Money and debt.** The top bar shows supplies and credits with the net per day under each. The **LEDGER** tab has the accounts for each of the last seven days and the week: what came in and went out, and from where. Upkeep is paid every day, and citizens are paid 2 credits a day each. Credits may go below zero to an overdraft (800 on Veteran), with a weekly fee; past that, wages go unpaid, franchise sales stop and morale drops until you are back in credit. Loans: the Founding Charter, a Bureau Development Loan (1,200) and Emergency Credit (500), each with weekly interest and a weekly repayment, up to 6,000 owed in all. Repay early from the Ledger. The Bureau also sells supplies (100 for 60 credits) and buys them back (100 for 35).
- **Leave it running.** A base you stop touching doesn't fall apart: deployment orders are sized to what you shipped last week and partly delivered orders still count, so it holds steady and simply stops growing. What brings decline is ignoring things.
- **Calls from the Ministries.** Now and then a Ministry asks the base to set something aside: ammunition for a front, rations for a relief column, E-710 for the fleet, credits for war bonds. A card comes up; answer within a few days and you gain standing. Let it lapse and that Ministry marks you down.
- **Supplies and credits.** Supplies build, pave and feed the base: they come from the weekly allocation, shipped troopers, won operations and fair roster grades. Credits are money: shipped troopers, special operations, franchise sales. Credits pay for research and Ledger requests, and buy supplies from the Bureau (100 for 60). The **LEDGER** tab's accounts show where both came from this week and last.
- **The Base Ledger.** During duty hours citizens and officials put requests on your desk: leave, a sign for the canteen, a sponsored bugle call, a quiet word from the inspector. A card slides in; open the LEDGER tab and choose. Each answer costs or earns something. Requests left two days file themselves, and people notice.
- **Roster review.** When a trooper finishes training their file comes up: range card, course time, a conduct note, missions and medals. Give the grade they have earned, A to D. A fair grade pays 8 supplies and 3 credits; a wild one is audited. A troopers are worth half as much again in credits when shipped; D goes back to training. Five files a day.
- **Roads and walls, one tool.** Pick Dirt Road, Paved Road (Expansion bar) or Wall (Defence bar) and drag from one end to the other. SNAP (or `G`) switches between grid, 45° and free; ends always join existing roads and walls. Dirt is one tile wide and jams when vehicles share it; paved is two wide and fast. Bulldoze takes a line out for half its cost. Vehicles keep to roads and crawl off them.
- **Plots.** Buildings go on roadside plots: pick one and green cells show where it fits; it snaps to the nearest, turned to face the road with its door on the street. Flags, banners, potties, vending machines, loudspeakers, solar panels and lookouts go anywhere inside the wall.
- **A defensible base.** Whatever your walls enclose is the base: draw new wall further out, then bulldoze the old to grow. A wall drawn across water stops at each shore. A road through a wall becomes a checkpoint gate and takes one trained trooper. Lookout Towers watch nine tiles of wall. The Ministry of Defence reviews the perimeter every week (Defence tab, BASE DEFENCE): watched wall, manned gates, few entrances, and the HQ and fuel kept away from the wall.
- **Three kinds of people.** SEAF troopers (blue) keep an Army day. Helldivers (black and gold, caped) thaw at a Helldiver Barracks and keep a Marine's: PT at 05:30, Colors at 08:00. Citizens staff the mess, the franchises, the lab and the offices, drive the trucks and forklifts, put a hand over their heart for the flag and watch the evening broadcast.
- **Medics.** Troopers hit on patrol are treated in a Medical Bay and return to duty; without a free bed most are flown off planet or lost.
- **Bigger buildings.** Research unlocks the Hab Block (thirty bunks, three storeys), the Broadcast Mast (four more pads) and the Command Tower (watches sixteen tiles of wall).
- **Landing pads and the Comms Tower.** Your first pad is free of paperwork. Every Comms Tower lets you run two more. Each pad gets its own pelican, so more pads mean bigger morning deliveries, more recruits and more seats on the afternoon transports.
- **Science.** A Science Lab (needs a Comms Tower) researches one upgrade at a time while it is staffed and powered: Long-Range Radio, Reinforced Shelving, Drill Sergeant Manuals, Patriotic Photovoltaics, Heavy-Lift Pelicans, a Third Truck, Bureau Asphalt, Fast Recon Vehicles and Armoured Doctrine. Pick projects in the **SCIENCE** tab; more labs research faster.
- **Briefings and armour.** Before any patrol leaves, the squad leader gathers his team by the Patrol Office (or the HQ) and gives them the plan, then they march out the gate together. With Fast Recon Vehicles researched, a Vehicle Bay keeps an FRV, and with Armoured Doctrine a tank. Tick them on a special operation card to take them along: better odds, fewer casualties, some fuel, and a two-day wait if one is wrecked. Armoured assaults only turn up once you have a Vehicle Bay.
- **Operations map and reports.** Every operation goes somewhere real: a named place on the planet, so many kilometres and which way, marked on the map in the Defence tab with the squads out in the field. Some missions call for an FRV (convoys, evacuations) or a tank (bug holes, bot outposts); bringing it gives better odds and a quarter more reward, and an Armoured assault really needs the tank. The FRV gets a squad there and back in two thirds of the time. Every special operation files an after-action report (standing patrols only when something happens): read them in the Defence tab.
- **Standing patrols.** A Patrol Office sends squads of three trained troopers out every morning at 08:00 and brings them home, on its own. Set how many squads it keeps out (0 to 3) when you select it. It keeps those troopers back from the transports. Special operations still turn up in the Defence panel if you want to send one by hand.
- **Three Ministries, three kinds of standing.** Bottom right, with what each has waiting.

  | Ministry | Wants | How it's scored |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Defence | Trained troopers shipped on time, patrols that come back | Each deployment order completed, each patrol won; a missed order costs standing |
  | Expansion | Supply lines that never run dry, and growth | A weekly audit: buildings stocked, votes fed, a bunk for everyone, and a base that keeps growing |
  | Truth | A base that inspires | A weekly inspection: morale (35%), propaganda (45%) and condition (20%) |

- **The week.** Seven days, two minutes each at normal speed. At the end of it Super Earth ranks the four factions by total standing and splits the allocation (1st gets 1.5×, 4th gets 0.6×), and the higher you rank the more of your empty bunks each morning's recruits fill. Then upkeep is paid.
- **Time.** The clock and date sit bottom left. `[` and `]` slow down and speed up (½× to 8×), `1` `2` `3` pick 1×, 2× and 4×, `Space` pauses.
- **The daily routine, Army style.** First Call at 06:00: everyone turns out in front of their quarters. Reveille at 06:30 as the flag goes up: following Army Regulation 600-25, everyone outdoors stops where they stand, faces the nearest flag and salutes, and vehicles halt; anyone indoors carries on. Then PT, chow at 08:00, Work Call at 09:00, mess at 11:30, duty until Retreat at 17:00 (the same honours as the flag comes down), free time, and Taps at 21:00, when everyone finds a bunk; votes without one sleep outdoors by the HQ. Generator and motor pool crews work nights. SEAF troopers wear light-blue armour and blue helmets; your faction colour goes on the armband and the crest on the shoulder (change it in the faction editor).
- **The flag plaza.** The open ground round the Super Earth flag is where medals are pinned and where everyone gathers for ceremonies. Select the flag to see the plaza and **MOVE FLAG** (100 supplies).
- **The Bureau** writes every week: memos, casualty form letters, and a ledger (STANDINGS) of the allocation before its handling fee and Presidential consulting. It counts votes, not people: "Two votes have been delivered to your base."
- **Fast food.** One chain per base by exclusive franchise: Big Liberty Burger (huge morale, huge upkeep), Patriot Clucks (cheap, but everyone works 8% slower) or Bureau Cola & Fries (little morale, but a weekly kickback). Each feeds 16.
- **Falling behind never ends the game.** A low rank means less supply, so upkeep goes unpaid and buildings wear down, morale slips and inspections suffer. You can always climb back.
- **Fort Sanctuary's buildings.** From the SESF base build: a Memorial Plaque at the gate, Floodlight Towers and Defence Turrets on the wall, the Emergency Operations Bunker, the Server and Storage Vault, the War Room, the Memorial Halls, DEMOCRA-MART, the Citizens' Casino, Cryopreservation Storage and the Security Brig. Most unlock through three new research projects (Fortification Doctrine, Civic Programme, Quantum Archives) and a fourth for the War Room.
- **Industry.** An E-710 Extractor (on a seep) pumps fuel, an Ore Mine (on ore) digs out supplies, and the State Industrial Complex (research State Industry, Fort or better) turns fuel into supplies. Each needs a crew and power.
- **Designs and parts.** Large buildings are designed before you place them. Pick one of three designs (Standard Issue; Fortified, which wears half as fast and adds Defence; Monumental, with columns, gold and banners for propaganda and pride), then add parts such as a lit sign, relay antennas, roof floodlights, a roof gun, solar panels or a holo display. Each part costs more and does more, and shows on the model. Fortified adds a walled yard a tile wide on each side and Monumental a colonnaded wing, so both need more ground. The Science Lab, Helldiver Barracks and Command Tower take designs too. Your last choice is kept for the next one.
- **Refits.** Select a designed building and press **REFIT** to change its design or parts. The ghost shows the new shape: green if it fits, red with the reason if not. A refit pays the difference in price plus a fee of 15% of the base price, and the building closes for about half a day while the work is done.
- **Families.** Trooper files list next of kin, and the Ledger sometimes brings a Form C-01 to countersign, a family visit or the Ministry of Humanity's census of dependants. Flavour, with small morale effects.
- **Sound.** Everything sounds from where it is. The ear sits at the camera: zoom in and the base gets louder, turn the view and the sounds turn with it. Every building type has its own sound (the generator hums, the range fires during duty hours, the mess clatters at mealtimes, the Medical Bay beeps, the comms mast crackles with Morse, flags flap), and so does every vehicle (gate barriers lifting, a squad marching out in step, truck engines and air brakes, the forklift's reversing beep, the FRV revving off, the tank's tracks, the pelican's turbines as it comes in over the base). Bugle calls come from the loudspeakers and the flag. Loudspeakers read out announcements in the browser's own voice, with a caption, and people near the camera say things in passing. The MENU has volume sliders for effects, base sounds, voices and music, and a switch for the spoken announcements.
- **Music.** Add your own tracks to the `music` folder and list them in `music/tracks.json` (see `music/README.md`). They play shuffled with pauses between.
- **Interface and graphics.** The interface matches Orbital Fury's sci-fi look (amber for what you spend, cyan for information); add `?ui=classic` to the address for the old green. GFX in the top bar sets graphics: by default it eases shadows and resolution off by itself when frames run slow.

## How it's built

Plain JavaScript modules, no framework and no build step. The 3D view is three.js, vendored as one file in `js/vendor/`. Every building, person, vehicle and crest is drawn in code, and there are no model or image files. The look (palette, type, panels and buttons) carries over from Orbital Fury and Super Orbital Simulator.

```
index.html          the page, the title, the faction editor, the HUD
css/style.css       the Orbital Fury theme plus the base-builder HUD
js/config.js        every tuning number: buildings, Ministries, missions, rivals, the week
js/sim.js           the simulation: buildings, crews, power, supply lines and vehicles, training,
                    deployments, patrols, audits, inspections, rivals, the week, saving (no DOM, no 3D)
js/path.js          road-aware pathfinding shared by vehicles and people
js/audio.js         positional sound, loudspeaker voices, the mixer and music
js/site.js          landing sites: biomes, the region map, terrain, water, rock and deposits
js/net.js           the road and wall network: snapping, junctions, gates
js/desk.js          the Commander's desk: Ledger requests, roster files, Ministry calls
js/voices.js        what the loudspeakers and people say
js/life.js          the ant farm: troopers, forklifts, pelicans, crates, inspectors, ceremonies
js/models.js        every building, person and vehicle, built from simple shapes
js/logo.js          the crest maker
js/view.js          the 3D view: rotating near-isometric camera, ground, the wire, picking
js/demo.js          the finished base behind the title screen
js/main.js          the loop, input, HUD, Ministries panel, faction editor, saves
js/rng.js, noise.js seeded random and value noise (from Super Orbital Simulator)
test/headless.mjs   plays a base without a browser and prints the standings week by week
test/ui.mjs         clicks through the real page on a desktop and on a phone held sideways
```

**Running your own copy:** serve this folder with any local web server (`npm start`, then open <http://localhost:8080>). Opening `index.html` straight from disk won't work, because browsers block ES modules on `file://`. Add `?play` to skip the title, and `&seed=N` to fix the map.

**Tests:** `npm run sim` needs only Node. It plays 14 weeks with a simple bot, and `npm run sim:idle` shows a base that builds nothing. `node test/headless.mjs 10 lapse` plays a base that is built and then left alone. For the browser test, run `npm install` and `npm start`, then `npm run ui` in another terminal.

**Saves:** the base saves on your device every 20 seconds and when you leave. **MENU → COPY SAVE CODE** gives you the whole base as text to keep or move to another device, and **LOAD SAVE CODE** brings it back.
